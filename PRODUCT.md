# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, founders, senior engineers, recruiters, and design-minded product teams evaluating Satyam Chaudhary for frontend, full-stack, or founding engineer roles. Visitors arrive with limited time and many portfolios to compare, so the site must communicate taste, engineering craft, and distinct point of view quickly.

## Product Purpose

This is a personal portfolio built to help Satyam stand out and get hired by showing proof of judgment, not just listing experience. The portfolio uses a newspaper and article-inspired format to frame work, metrics, writing, and personality as an authored editorial surface. Success means visitors remember the site, understand Satyam as a design-focused builder, and feel enough confidence to reach out.

## Brand Personality

Editorial, handcrafted, distinctive. The voice should feel confident and authored, like a builder's newspaper made by someone who cares about layout, type, detail, and shipping real products. It should feel personal without becoming casual, and experimental without losing clarity.

## Anti-references

Do not make this feel like another generic developer portfolio, template resume site, SaaS landing page, or polished but forgettable AI-startup page. Avoid copying another portfolio's surface treatment directly. The newspaper direction should feel owned by Satyam, not like a pasted reference.

## Design Principles

1. Make taste visible: the interface should prove design judgment before the visitor reads a word.
2. Treat work like editorial evidence: projects, roles, and metrics should read as reported stories, not cards in a template grid.
3. Stand apart without hiding the basics: distinct art direction should still make identity, experience, skills, and contact paths easy to find.
4. Preserve the handmade newspaper feeling: texture, rules, typography, rhythm, and motion should feel tactile and intentional.
5. Let the portfolio be memorable: take creative risks where they strengthen recall, especially in layout, type, and section pacing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal accessibility target is set right now. Preserve baseline usability where practical: readable contrast, keyboard-accessible links and controls, semantic structure, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion support for major animations.
